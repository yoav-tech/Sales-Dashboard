Good copy, five broken promises.

A full content audit of InfluencerMarketing.ai — every page, every headline, every meta description, plus the generated llms.txt — read against Problem · Agitate · Solution and Attention · Interest · Desire · Action.

The short version

The copy on this site is better than most B2B SaaS. Sixteen of 27 pages already run a disciplined, near-textbook PAS structure, and the agitate copy is specific and human — “A creator says yes in a DM you never see again.” Headline voice is consistent, concrete and benefit-led. That is not a small thing, and none of it needs rewriting.

The problems are not in the writing. They are in three places:

  • Six factual contradictions that break the AIDA “Action” step. The site promises a 7-day trial on some pages and 14-day on others; promises “no card required” on 18 pages and then asks for card details; quotes 400M+ creators in every meta description and 412M+ in eight more places; is actively selling a webinar that happened 29 days ago; and ships an llms.txt that states two of those contradictions in a single sentence.
  • The meta layer does zero PAS work. All 27 descriptions are pure Solution — feature lists with no Problem and no Agitate. Five also overflow and truncate, and two lose their strongest hook in the cut.
  • Eleven pages have no Problem/Agitate section at all — including platform, solutions, pricing and customers. customers.html, which should be the Desire engine of the whole funnel, has exactly two headlines.

Fix the six contradictions first. They cost money today and take an hour or two.

Where to make the changes

Headlines and body copy live in site/*.html plus the four onboarding pages at the repo root. Titles and meta descriptions live in the S={…} map in build_site.py (lines 43–99). A third file, build_extras.py, generates llms.txt, 404.html and the web manifest. The files under public/ are build output — they are wiped and regenerated, so never edit them directly.

01Critical defects

These six are not opinions. Each is verifiable in the source, and each breaks AIDA at the Action step — where it is most expensive.

The site cannot decide whether the trial is 7 or 14 days, and the contradiction lands hardest on the payment page itself.

Worst case: payment.html — the final step before payment — has 7-day in its own title and 14-day in its own H1. A prospect arriving from /solutions/smb (which says 7-day four times, with a Day-5/Day-7 timeline) sees a browser tab saying 7 and a headline saying 14. AIDA impact: Action. 1.2 P0 “No card required” is promised on 18 pages, then contradicted at checkout

Eighteen pages carry the phrase “no card required” (or “no credit card”): agencies, ai-agents, campaign-management, competitive-intelligence, consumer-intelligence, creator-payouts, discovery, ecommerce, enterprise, index, influencer-crm, llm-visibility, pr, pricing, smb, solutions, tracking-roi, ugc.

Then payment.html opens with the H1: “Enter card details to activate your 14-day trial.”

AIDA impact: Action, catastrophically. The single most repeated risk-reversal on the site is revoked at the moment of commitment. Worse, the H1 leads with the friction instead of the reward — a textbook conversion anti-pattern. The button beneath it (“Pay $0 and start your 14-day trial”) gets this exactly right; the headline above it does not.

FixIf a card is required, remove “no card required” from all 18 pages — it is a false promise. If it is not, this page is mislabelled and should be reframed as an optional upgrade step. Either way, rewrite the H1 to lead with reward: “Start your 14-day trial. You won't be charged today.” — with card entry as the mechanism beneath, not the headline.

1.4 P0 The creator count contradicts itself — including within the homepage

400M+ appears around 90 times, across every page and every meta description. 412M+ appears in 10 places across 6 source files: index.html lines 150, 157 and 246; pricing.html lines 8 and 1501; discovery.html line 36; platform.html line 54; smb.html line 43; and build_site.py lines 53 and 93 — plus the generated llms.txt.

Which means the homepage's own meta description says 400M+ while its hero proof-point says 412M+.

AIDA impact: Interest and Desire. This is the site's single most-repeated credibility number. Two versions of it means neither is trusted. Note that find-influencers.html breaks down as 218M + 112M + 58M = 388M across three platforms — coherent with 400M+, less so with 412M+.

FixPick one and propagate everywhere. If the database genuinely grew from 400M to 412M, update all ~90 instances — a stale number repeated 90 times is worse than a new number stated once.

1.6 P0 llms.txt states two contradictions in one sentence — and it is the file AI reads

build_extras.py:83 generates public/llms.txt, the machine-readable brief that ChatGPT, Perplexity and other AI crawlers read to learn what IMAI is. Its “Key facts” section contains this line verbatim:

Pricing: Personal $129/mo, Starter $299/mo, Growth $499/mo, Scale $1,200/mo, and custom Enterprise. 7-day free trial, no card required. Full 412M+ creator database on every plan.

That single sentence carries both the trial contradiction (§1.1 — 7-day, against 14-day on pricing and register) and the creator-count contradiction (§1.4 — 412M+, against 400M+ used four times elsewhere in the same file). The document contradicts itself internally: 400M+ appears four times and 412M+ once, inside one 4 KB file.

AIDA impact: upstream of all four stages. This file shapes what AI assistants say about IMAI before a buyer ever reaches the site — and an assistant summarizing a self-contradicting source will either pick a number arbitrarily or hedge.

Why this one stings: IMAI sells LLM Visibility as a product — “Own how AI talks about you”, “Influence the sources AI cites”, “Wrong or stale facts: AI repeats outdated claims and you have no lever to correct them.” The company's own AI-facing source file has exactly the defect its product exists to fix. Any prospect who opens influencermarketing.ai/llms.txt before buying that product will notice.

FixResolve §1.1 and §1.4, then regenerate. Better: derive the trial length and creator count in build_extras.py from single named constants shared with build_site.py, so the two files cannot drift apart again.

02The meta layer

All 27 descriptions are Solution-only. Not one opens with a Problem or an Agitate. Every one reads as a feature manifest.

This is the biggest single missed opportunity on the site, because the SERP snippet is where PAS pays best — you are competing for a click against nine other results, and “here is what we do” loses to “here is what is going wrong for you.”

The irony is that the pages already contain the hooks. tracking-roi.html opens with “‘It went well.’ Prove it.” — and its meta description says “Measure influencer ROI with sales attribution via SDK, UTMs and discount codes.” The PAS line was already written. It just never made it into the meta layer.

Titles are mostly solid for head terms but are category labels with no Attention value. The recommendations below keep the head keyword and add tension only where the character budget allows. Bars show length against the 155-character SERP limit, marked.

Shared defect B — the Action path argues with itself

Four solution pages — ai-agents, consumer-intelligence, llm-visibility and ugc — close with “Book a demo” + “See pricing”. The other eleven close with “Start free trial” + “Book a demo”.

But all four also carry “Start free trial” in the hero and “no card required” in the trust bar. So the page opens by offering a trial and closes by withholding it, sending the highest-intent visitor to a pricing table instead. Either these four products genuinely have no self-serve trial — in which case remove the trial CTA and the “no card required” line from their heroes — or they do, and the closing CTA should match the other eleven.

The eleven pages with no Problem or Agitate

These are not bad pages. They are half-built ones — they skip straight to Solution.

platform.html PAS D · AIDA C

The hero deck contains a genuine problem statement — “Stop stitching together a database, an inbox, a spreadsheet and a payments tool” — and then the page never agitates it. It goes straight into a feature list: six capability H3s, then six more under “AI that does the heavy lifting.” Structurally it is a spec sheet with a good opening line.

Separately, the first H2 is “Pay creators in 135+ currencies.” — a single feature occupying the most prominent section headline on the whole-platform page. Payouts are one of six capabilities; this is a hierarchy error.

FixInsert a Problem/Agitate section after the hero, reusing the four agitate cards index.html already has — Databases / Email inboxes / Spreadsheets / Separate payments. They were written for exactly this argument. Then demote the payouts H2 to peer level with the other capabilities.

customers.html PAS F · AIDA D

This page has two headlines in total: “Real results from real campaigns.” and “Ready to replicate these results?” That is the entire headline inventory.

Customer proof is the Desire engine of the whole funnel — it is where a skeptical buyer goes to be convinced, and every other page routes here. Two headlines and a logo wall cannot carry that weight.

Give each an H2 that states an outcome rather than a label — “Estée Lauder cut creator vetting from 3 weeks to 2 days.” Right now the page asserts results without ever narrating them.

The remaining nine follow the same pattern in milder form:

  • find-influencers.html — PAS C+. Excellent Interest and Desire content, but “One database, every network.” is a Solution in the Problem slot. Add: “Six networks, six tools, six shortlists that never talk.”
  • pricing.html — PAS C. The comparison table does real PAS work implicitly, which is the page's strength, but there is no explicit Problem. Lead with the cost of the status quo: “Cheaper than the four tools it replaces.”
  • about.html — PAS D+. “AI does the heavy lifting — people make the calls.” is good. The values trio is generic and interchangeable with any SaaS about page. Values headings should carry a claim, not a noun: “Transparency” → “Real audience data, or we don't show it.”
